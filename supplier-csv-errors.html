5 Supplier CSV Errors to Check Before Updating Your Online Store

A simple checklist to spot issues in supplier price lists before making changes to your product catalogue.

Updating product data from a supplier CSV file may seem straightforward. You receive the latest price list, open it in Excel, and prepare the data for your store.

But small problems in supplier files can lead to incorrect costs, mismatched products or extra manual work. Before importing a new file, review these five common issues.

1. Duplicate SKUs

A SKU (Stock Keeping Unit) is often used to identify a product. When the same SKU appears more than once, it may be unclear which row is correct.

What to check: Find repeated SKUs and review whether they are errors or valid supplier-specific entries.

2. Missing Product Identifiers

Rows with empty SKUs can be difficult to match to existing products. Don't assume a product name alone is a reliable identifier.

What to check: Review rows with blank or missing product identifiers before matching or updating products.

3. Missing or Invalid Prices

Prices may be blank, zero or stored as text where a number is expected. Such values deserve a manual check rather than automatic acceptance.

What to check: Flag empty, zero and non-numeric cost values, and verify suspicious entries with the supplier.

4. Unexpected Price Increases

Supplier prices do change. But if a product's cost moves from €12 to €120, it could indicate a different pack size, a genuine adjustment or a data-entry error.

What to check: Compare old and new costs by SKU and investigate unusually large changes.

5. Products Missing From the Latest File

A product present in the previous price list might disappear from the latest version. That does not necessarily mean it is discontinued: it could be out of stock, renamed or accidentally omitted.

What to check: Compare SKU lists across file versions, and review missing products before taking action.

Quick supplier CSV checklist

  • Are there duplicate or missing SKUs?
  • Are cost values present and valid?
  • Have any prices changed unexpectedly?
  • Which SKUs are new?
  • Which SKUs have disappeared?

Reviewing these exceptions before importing can help prevent avoidable mistakes. Always check your store platform's import requirements separately.
